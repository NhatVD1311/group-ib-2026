Outsider PhaaS Hitting Serbia
[]

In June 2026 Google filed a lawsuit targeting "Outsider Enterprise," a Phishing-as-a-Service (PhaaS) platform based in China, developed by the threat actor known as Chenlun, also known as Sinkinto (better known under the Telegram handles @chenlun and @sinkinto01). The lawsuit resulted in the FBI seizing several domains of main admin servers, as well as a Shopify e-commerce storefront and account used to test the phishing service; approximately $100K USDT from Outsider payment wallets; thousands of phishing domains from U.S. providers, rerouting them to an FBI splash page; and leveraged an Outsider Telegram bot to obtain information on Outsider customers. According to public reporting, Outsider was responsible for at least an estimated 3.8 million stolen credit cards corresponding to an estimated $1.9B in losses. Unfortunately, similar to other PhaaS platforms recently disrupted by law enforcement — such as Tycoon 2FA — the threat persists and continues to actively target organizations worldwide. Group-IB’s Threat Intelligence team has identified over 700 new phishing pages created within just one month after the civil lawsuit, indicating that affiliates continue to actively utilize the Outsider kit. Among the more than 260 templates available in Outsider, two target Serbia — a country that has been targeted in the past as well, according to internal telemetry and analysis of the phishing kit's available templates, which mimicked the Serbian branch of Banca Intesa, and the national postal service, Pošta Srbije, under the following template names: Template Name Translation 积分类-塞尔维亚-BancaIntesa RS-Points-BancaIntesa 包裹类-塞尔维亚-posta RS-POST-posta The Croatian national postal service also had its own template. In addition, a year ago, in September 2025, a custom template was observed targeting Serbian individuals and mimicking a company dubbed serbiatravelservice[.]com. Fraudsters created a domain under “icu” gTLD (serbiatravelservice[.]icu) and targeted Serbian individuals: Moj mts, operated by Telekom Srbija was also observed being impersonated by Oursider PhaaS: The Outsider PhaaS has already been technically detailed in two reports recently published by Group-IB: Chenlun's Outsider PhaaS Kit Smishing Triad & Outsider JWR This report aims to complement those publications by providing additional analysis and context not previously disclosed. Readers seeking further technical detail are encouraged to consult the blogs referenced above, which will also be briefly cited throughout Final Goal: Payment Details The goal of Outsider PhaaS is to steal payment details or gain access to payment or credit card details and financial platforms such as PayPal, Brokerage firms or bank portals. The stolen information is not limited to credit card numbers, expiry dates, and security codes (CVV), but also includes other details necessary to make that payment information actionable — such as data used to bypass MFA (3DS), authorize the addition of a payment method to a mobile device, or complete a purchase directly on behalf of the fraudster. As described in the blog, Outsider PhaaS has the capability to capture the following fields from the victims: Credit Card Data: Card number, holder name, expiration date, CVV, and PIN. Account Logins: Website login accounts/passwords, bank portal credentials, and PayPal login strings. Personal Identity: First/last name, phone number, physical address, email addresses, Date of Birth, and Social Security Numbers (SSN). Security Bypass Elements: Fields to catch SMS verification codes, 2FA prompt characters, and custom validation errors. Attack Flow The attack flow is well described in Group-IB’s blog. Victims are targeted with an SMS although iMessage has also been observed being leveraged by threat actors behind this fraud scheme. Attackers use different lures. Taking into account the wide variety of already created templates Outsider has, in addition to the customization capabilities offered by the PhaaS make the potential schemes infinite. Google has confirmed threat actors behind Outsider have been observed using Gemini to create templates. From fake delivery lures mimicking USPS and similar companies, to fake gift redemption pages from big corporations reward programs to directly impersonating targeted victim’s bank in regards to restoring an account access. Once the login or payment details are captured, the operator then dynamically serves contextual 2FA challenges, including SMS verification, email verification, PIN entry, or app-based authentication, based on the institution’s expected authentication requirements. Operators are observed to retain the capability to redirect victims back to the payment page to solicit additional card details, enabling multi-card harvesting from a single victim. Example of operator ability to redirect victims to solve MFA challenge Criminal Structure Outsider’s business model is a subscription model. They serve the PhaaS and the subscribers pay a fee. The Outsider platform provides cyber criminals infrastructure hosting phishing website files and resources via “phishing kits”. However, how do Outsider customers, phishing operators, monetize their activities? There is a whole cybercriminal structure that allows all involved parties to run their criminal business successfully. On November 15, 2025 Chenlun created an exclusive, members only group for affiliates that purchased the phishing kit. As of April 2026 there were 220 members. Recommendations 1. Customer-facing anti-fraud controls Kill "OTP as sole friction." Outsider dynamically relays whatever 2FA challenge the bank serves (SMS, app push, PIN) in real time to the victim. Push to app-based, out-of-band authentication with transaction-binding (amount/merchant shown in the approval prompt) rather than a numeric code that can be phished and replayed. Step up friction on "add card to wallet" events. The Theft Group's monetization path explicitly includes loading stolen cards to digital wallets (Google Wallet named). Recommend the bank tighten card-provisioning risk scoring (device binding, velocity checks, out-of-band confirmation call/push) specifically for wallet-add attempts, not just for payments. Rate-limit and flag repeat card entry in one session. The kit redirects victims back to the payment page to harvest multiple cards — a customer submitting several distinct PANs in a short window post-"login" is itself a signal worth correlating against fraud systems, even outside the phishing context. 2. Detection & monitoring (brand/domain protection) Operationalize the TLD/ASN pattern, not just exact-match brand monitoring. Post-seizure infra clusters on Cloudflare (AS13335, fronting) with Alibaba (AS45102)/Tencent (AS132203) origins, .top/.shop/.icu/.cc TLDs, and Dominet (HK) as registrar. Feed these into your domain-monitoring/typosquat detection rules (weighted scoring, not blocklist) since new Outsider infrastructure will keep following this fingerprint even as domains rotate. Monitor for Serbian-language template reuse — the templates are pre-built and traded among Outsider's 220+ affiliates, so expect recurrence/variants, not a one-off campaign. Watch for AI-generated lure variety. Google confirmed Gemini-assisted template creation — expect faster iteration and more convincing localized lures than typical smishing kits. Static content-matching detection (fixed strings/screenshots) will degrade faster than usual; favor structural/behavioral detection. Track new pages utilizing the unique file name signatures to initiate takedown of phishing sites Integrate our Threat Intelligence solution to identify emerging phishing kits early Implement continuous monitoring for SMS-linked brand abuse with a comprehensive Digital Risk Protection solution. 3. SOC / threat intel actions Ingest the IOCs as pivots, use attacker-side infrastructure fingerprints (used for their own ad/traffic ops) — useful for correlating future campaigns even after domains rotate. Send these to your threat intel/OSINT pivoting workflow. Track the exfil field schema for detection engineering: the kit specifically targets PAN/CVV/PIN, bank/PayPal credentials, SSN/DOB, and MFA codes together. Any customer-reported phishing site collecting that exact combination should be triaged as Outsider-linked and prioritized for fast takedown given the 2FA-relay capability (time-sensitive — this isn't a static credential harvester). Coordinate takedown requests targeting the hosting layer, not just the domain, given the heavy Cloudflare fronting — domain takedown alone won't kill the origin; escalate to Alibaba/Tencent abuse teams in parallel.
